# k-NN regression with several standardized inputs, as in the lecture: squared distances by
# matrix algebra, then the mean outcome of the k nearest training rows.
knn_predict <- function(Xnew, Xtrain, ytrain, k) {
  d2 <- outer(rowSums(Xnew^2), rowSums(Xtrain^2), "+") - 2 * Xnew %*% t(Xtrain)
  apply(d2, 1, function(d) mean(ytrain[order(d)[1:k]]))
}

Before the seminar

The lecture stated one result, the optimism theorem, and left its proof to you (exercise 1). It used ridge regression without deriving its estimator (exercise 2). Do both on paper before the seminar; the seminar’s problems are variations on them. You may use, without proof: linearity of expectation; the tower property, \(\mathbb E[W] = \mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[W \mid V]\big]\); that for a symmetric matrix \(\mathbf A\) the gradient of \(\beta^T\mathbf A\beta\) is \(2\mathbf A\beta\) and its Hessian is \(2\mathbf A\); and that a symmetric matrix has real eigenvalues and an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors. Throughout, assume that every expectation that appears exists and every minimizer named exists.

Exercise 1 (optimism theorem). Training data \((x_i, y_i)\), \(i = 1, \ldots, n\), independent draws from the population of \((X, Y)\); a loss \(\ell\); the risk \(R(f) = \mathbb E\,\ell(Y, f(X))\) and the training error \(\hat R_n(f) = \tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \ell(y_i, f(x_i))\). A class \(\mathcal F\) is fixed before the data; \(f_{\mathcal F}\) minimizes \(R\) over \(\mathcal F\); \(\hat f\) minimizes \(\hat R_n\) over \(\mathcal F\) (the empirical risk minimizer). Expectations are over the training data.

  1. Let \(f\) be any fixed function, chosen without looking at the data. Show that \(\mathbb E[\hat R_n(f)] = R(f)\). Which part of “independent draws from the population” did you use, and which part did you not use?

  2. Show that \(\hat R_n(\hat f) \le \hat R_n(f_{\mathcal F})\) for every training set, and deduce \(\mathbb E[\hat R_n(\hat f)] \le R(f_{\mathcal F})\).

  3. Show that \(R(f_{\mathcal F}) \le R(\hat f)\) for every training set, and deduce \(R(f_{\mathcal F}) \le \mathbb E[R(\hat f)]\). Of the two inequalities in the theorem, which used the sampling assumption and which used only the definitions?

  4. A test set \((x'_j, y'_j)\), \(j = 1, \ldots, m\), of independent draws from the population, independent of the training data, scores \(\hat f\) by \(\hat R_{\text{test}}(\hat f) = \tfrac1m\sum_j \ell(y'_j, \hat f(x'_j))\). Show that \(\mathbb E[\hat R_{\text{test}}(\hat f)] = \mathbb E[R(\hat f)]\): the test score lands, on average, on the right end of the theorem.

NoteHint 1
  1. \(\hat R_n(f)\) is an average of \(n\) terms; take the expectation of each term. What is the distribution of \((x_i, y_i)\)?
NoteHint 2
  1. \(\hat f\) minimizes \(\hat R_n\) over \(\mathcal F\), and \(f_{\mathcal F}\) is a member of \(\mathcal F\). Then apply (a) to \(f_{\mathcal F}\): is it a fixed function?
NoteHint 3
  1. Condition on the training data. Given them, \(\hat f\) is a fixed function and the test rows are still draws from the population; apply (a), then the tower property.

Exercise 2 (ridge regression’s normal equations). \(\mathbf X\) is \(n \times p\) with centered columns and \(\mathbf y\) is centered, so there is no intercept to fit (the lecture’s convention: the intercept is not penalized). For \(\lambda > 0\) the ridge estimator minimizes \[ Q_\lambda(\beta) = \|\mathbf y - \mathbf X\beta\|^2 + \lambda\|\beta\|^2. \]

  1. Expand \(Q_\lambda\), compute its gradient, and show that a stationary point satisfies \((\mathbf X^T\mathbf X + \lambda\mathbf I)\beta = \mathbf X^T\mathbf y\).

  2. Show that \(\mathbf X^T\mathbf X + \lambda\mathbf I\) is positive definite, hence invertible, for every \(\lambda > 0\), whatever \(n\) and \(p\) are. Conclude that \(\hat\beta_\lambda = (\mathbf X^T\mathbf X + \lambda\mathbf I)^{-1}\mathbf X^T\mathbf y\) is the unique stationary point, even when \(p > n\).

  3. Compute the Hessian of \(Q_\lambda\). What are its smallest and largest curvatures, in terms of the eigenvalues \(\mu_1 \ge \dots \ge \mu_p \ge 0\) of \(\mathbf X^T\mathbf X\)? Why is the stationary point the global minimizer, and what does the penalty do to the ratio of largest to smallest curvature, the ratio that sets the speed of gradient descent?

  4. Show that \(\|\hat\beta_\lambda\| \le \|\mathbf X^T\mathbf y\|/\lambda\), so \(\hat\beta_\lambda \to \mathbf 0\) as \(\lambda \to \infty\). What is \(\hat\beta_\lambda\) at \(\lambda = 0\) when \(\mathbf X^T\mathbf X\) is invertible?

NoteHint 1
  1. \(\|\mathbf y - \mathbf X\beta\|^2 = \mathbf y^T\mathbf y - 2\beta^T\mathbf X^T\mathbf y + \beta^T\mathbf X^T\mathbf X\beta\), and \(\|\beta\|^2 = \beta^T\mathbf I\beta\). Differentiate term by term.
NoteHint 2
  1. For any \(v\), \(v^T\mathbf X^T\mathbf X v = \|\mathbf X v\|^2\). What is \(v^T(\mathbf X^T\mathbf X + \lambda\mathbf I)v\) when \(v \ne \mathbf 0\)?
NoteHint 3

(c), (d) \(\mathbf X^T\mathbf X\) and \(\mathbf X^T\mathbf X + \lambda\mathbf I\) have the same eigenvectors; the eigenvalues of the second are \(\mu_j + \lambda\). Write \(\mathbf X^T\mathbf y\) in the eigenvector basis.

Problems

Problem 1 (the selection winner’s score). \(K\) candidate predictors \(f_1, \ldots, f_K\) have been fitted on the training rows, say by \(K\) methods or one method at \(K\) values of its knob. A selection set of \(m\) rows, independent draws from the population and independent of the training rows, scores them: \(S_k = \tfrac1m\sum_j \ell(y_j, f_k(x_j))\). The winner is \(\hat k = \arg\min_k S_k\), and \(S_{\hat k}\) is its score, the number at the top of the leaderboard.

  1. Show that \(\mathbb E[S_k \mid \text{training data}] = R(f_k)\) for each \(k\).

  2. Show that \(\mathbb E[S_{\hat k} \mid \text{training data}] \le \min_k R(f_k) \le \mathbb E[R(f_{\hat k}) \mid \text{training data}]\). Which argument from exercise 1 is this, with what in place of \(\mathcal F\) and of \(\hat R_n\)?

  3. Two candidates with the same risk \(r\), whose scores are independent and each equal to \(r - 1\) or \(r + 1\) with probability \(\tfrac12\). Compute \(\mathbb E[S_{\hat k}]\) and \(\mathbb E[R(f_{\hat k})]\). Now \(K\) such candidates: what is \(\mathbb E[S_{\hat k}]\), and what happens as \(K\) grows?

  4. The winner is then scored once on the test set. Why does that score not have the same problem?

Problem 2 (where the proof breaks). The optimism theorem does not cover every method. Its proof needs \(\hat f\) to be the minimizer of the training error over a class fixed before the data, and that fails for ridge regression at a fixed \(\lambda\), for a tree grown greedily, and for any knob chosen on the data (problem 1). The first two cases:

  1. A population in which \(Y = X\) exactly (no noise), \(\mathbb E X^2 > 0\), squared loss, and the class \(\mathcal F = \{x \mapsto \beta x : \beta \in \mathbb R\}\) of lines through the origin. Find \(f_{\mathcal F}\) and \(R(f_{\mathcal F})\). For a sample \(x_1, \ldots, x_n\) (not all zero) and a fixed \(\lambda > 0\), find the ridge estimate \(\hat\beta_\lambda\) and its training error \(\hat R_n(\hat\beta_\lambda)\), and show that \(\mathbb E[\hat R_n(\hat\beta_\lambda)] > R(f_{\mathcal F})\): the left inequality fails. Which hypothesis of the theorem does \(\hat\beta_\lambda\) violate?

  2. Replace the penalty by the constraint \(|\beta| \le t\) for a fixed \(t > 0\): \(\mathcal F_t = \{x \mapsto \beta x : |\beta| \le t\}\), and \(\hat f\) the minimizer of \(\hat R_n\) over \(\mathcal F_t\). Does the theorem apply? What is \(\hat f\) here, for \(t \ge 1\) and for \(t < 1\)?

  3. A tree of depth \(2\) grown greedily (the lecture’s splitting) is a member of the class of all depth-\(2\) trees on the same inputs. Which line of the proof is not available for it? Does that mean its training error must sit above its risk on average, or only that the theorem does not say?

  4. Which of the theorem’s two inequalities holds for every \(\hat f \in \mathcal F\), whatever algorithm produced it?

Problem 3 (ridge as least squares on more rows). Stack \(p\) extra rows under the data: \(\tilde{\mathbf X} = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf X \\ \sqrt\lambda\,\mathbf I_p\end{pmatrix}\), an \((n + p) \times p\) matrix, and \(\tilde{\mathbf y} = \begin{pmatrix}\mathbf y \\ \mathbf 0_p\end{pmatrix}\).

  1. Show that \(\|\tilde{\mathbf y} - \tilde{\mathbf X}\beta\|^2 = \|\mathbf y - \mathbf X\beta\|^2 + \lambda\|\beta\|^2\), the ridge objective.

  2. Write the least-squares normal equations for the regression of \(\tilde{\mathbf y}\) on \(\tilde{\mathbf X}\) and check that they are ridge regression’s, \((\mathbf X^T\mathbf X + \lambda\mathbf I)\beta = \mathbf X^T\mathbf y\). Show that \(\tilde{\mathbf X}\) has full column rank for every \(\lambda > 0\), even when \(p > n\).

  3. In words: what does each of the \(p\) extra rows say, and what does \(\sqrt\lambda\) do? How would you fit ridge regression with nothing but lm()?
