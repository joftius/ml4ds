Before the seminar

The lecture proved one result, the descent lemma, which you prove again with the slides closed (exercise 1). It stated the ERM form of the bias–variance decomposition and left its two-line proof to you (exercise 2), set the Newton-Raphson exercise on the logistic log-likelihood (exercise 3), and stated no free lunch (exercise 4, a proof on a finite domain). Do all four on paper before the seminar; the seminar’s problems are variations on them. You may use, without proof: the fundamental theorem of calculus along a line, \(f(y) - f(x) = \int_0^1 \nabla f(x + t(y - x))^T (y - x)\, dt\) for a differentiable \(f\); the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality \(|a^T b| \le \|a\|\,\|b\|\); and the tower property, \(\mathbb E[W] = \mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[W \mid X]\big]\). Throughout, assume that every expectation that appears exists.

Exercise 1 (descent lemma). Let \(f: \mathbb R^p \to \mathbb R\) be differentiable with an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient: \(\|\nabla f(x) - \nabla f(y)\| \le L\,\|x - y\|\) for all \(x, y\), for some \(L > 0\). Fix \(x\), a step size \(\gamma > 0\), and write \(y = x - \gamma\nabla f(x)\) and \(v = y - x\).

  1. Write \(f(y) - f(x)\) as an integral along the segment from \(x\) to \(y\), and split it into \(\nabla f(x)^T v\) plus a remainder.

  2. Bound the remainder by \(\tfrac{L}{2}\|v\|^2\).

  3. Substitute \(v = -\gamma\nabla f(x)\) and show that \(f(y) \le f(x) - \gamma\big(1 - \tfrac12 L\gamma\big)\|\nabla f(x)\|^2\). For which \(\gamma\) is the right-hand side below \(f(x)\), and what does \(\gamma = 1/L\) give?

  4. The lemma says each step decreases \(f\). Give a function with an \(L\)-Lipschitz gradient for which the iterates \(x_{t+1} = x_t - \nabla f(x_t)/L\) never converge, and say in one sentence what the lemma does and does not guarantee.

NoteHint 1
  1. Apply the fundamental theorem of calculus along the line to \(f\), with \(x_t = x + tv\). To split, add and subtract \(\nabla f(x)^T v\) inside the integral.
NoteHint 2
  1. Cauchy-Schwarz on \([\nabla f(x_t) - \nabla f(x)]^T v\), then the Lipschitz bound, then \(\|x_t - x\| = t\|v\|\). What is \(\int_0^1 t\, dt\)?
NoteHint 3
  1. Think of a function with no minimum at all. Its gradient is as Lipschitz as you like.

Exercise 2 (bias–variance decomposition, ERM form). Risk \(R(f) = \mathbb E\,\ell(Y, f(X))\). Let \(f^\star\) minimize \(R\) over all predictors, let \(\mathcal F\) be a class of predictors with a best member \(f_{\mathcal F} = \arg\min_{f \in \mathcal F} R(f)\), and let \(\hat f \in \mathcal F\) be the predictor an algorithm returns from the sample (the empirical risk minimizer, or anything else in \(\mathcal F\)).

  1. Show that \(R(\hat f) - R(f_{\mathcal F}) \ge 0\) and \(R(f_{\mathcal F}) - R(f^\star) \ge 0\).

  2. Show that \(R(\hat f) = R(f^\star) + \big[R(\hat f) - R(f_{\mathcal F})\big] + \big[R(f_{\mathcal F}) - R(f^\star)\big]\), and say which term is random and which is fixed once \(\mathcal F\) is chosen.

  3. For squared loss, use week 2’s optimal model theorem to write the approximation term as a squared distance.

  4. The smallest class there is, \(\mathcal F = \{\text{constant functions}\}\), squared loss, \(\hat f = \bar y\) from an i.i.d. sample of size \(n\). Find \(f_{\mathcal F}\), show that the approximation error is \(\mathrm{Var}(f^\star(X))\), and show that the expected estimation error is \(\mathrm{Var}(Y)/n\).

NoteHint 1
  1. What does “\(f_{\mathcal F}\) minimizes \(R\) over \(\mathcal F\)” say about \(R(\hat f)\), given that \(\hat f \in \mathcal F\)?
NoteHint 2
  1. The optimal model theorem: \(R(f) - R(f^\star) = \mathbb E[(f^\star(X) - f(X))^2]\) for every \(f\). Apply it to \(f = f_{\mathcal F}\).
NoteHint 3
  1. For a constant \(c\), \(R(c) = \mathbb E(Y - c)^2 = \mathrm{Var}(Y) + (c - \mathbb E Y)^2\). Then the optimal model theorem once more, and \(\mathrm{Var}(Y) = \mathbb E[\mathrm{Var}(Y \mid X)] + \mathrm{Var}(\mathbb E[Y \mid X])\).

Exercise 3 (score equations, Hessian, Newton-Raphson). Logistic regression with design matrix \(\mathbf X\) (\(n\) rows \(\mathbf x_i^T\)), labels \(y_i \in \{0, 1\}\), and \(p_i(\beta) = 1/(1 + e^{-\mathbf x_i^T\beta})\).

  1. Show that the log-likelihood can be written \(\ell(\beta) = \sum_i \big[y_i\,\mathbf x_i^T\beta - \log(1 + e^{\mathbf x_i^T\beta})\big]\), and that its gradient is \(\nabla\ell(\beta) = \sum_i \mathbf x_i\,(y_i - p_i(\beta)) = \mathbf X^T(\mathbf y - \mathbf p(\beta))\).

  2. Differentiate once more to get the Hessian \(\nabla^2\ell(\beta) = -\sum_i p_i(1 - p_i)\,\mathbf x_i\mathbf x_i^T = -\mathbf X^T\mathbf W\mathbf X\) with \(\mathbf W = \mathrm{diag}(p_i(1 - p_i))\).

  3. Show that \(v^T\nabla^2\ell(\beta)\,v \le 0\) for every \(v\), so \(\ell\) is concave, and say when the inequality is strict for every \(v \ne 0\).

  4. Newton-Raphson finds a root of \(\nabla\ell\) by solving the linear approximation \(\nabla\ell(\beta_t) + \nabla^2\ell(\beta_t)(\beta - \beta_t) = \mathbf 0\) for \(\beta\). Write the update \(\beta_{t+1}\) in terms of \(\mathbf X\), \(\mathbf W_t\), \(\mathbf y\) and \(\mathbf p_t\).

NoteHint 1
  1. \(\log p_i = -\log(1 + e^{-\mathbf x_i^T\beta})\) and \(\log(1 - p_i) = -\mathbf x_i^T\beta - \log(1 + e^{-\mathbf x_i^T\beta})\). For the gradient, \(\tfrac{d}{dz}\log(1 + e^z) = e^z/(1 + e^z)\), which is \(p_i\) at \(z = \mathbf x_i^T\beta\).
NoteHint 2
  1. \(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial\beta} p_i(\beta) = p_i(1 - p_i)\,\mathbf x_i\). (c) \(v^T\mathbf x_i\mathbf x_i^T v = (\mathbf x_i^T v)^2\).

Exercise 4 (no free lunch on a finite domain). A finite set \(\mathcal X\) has \(N\) points. A labeling is a function \(f: \mathcal X \to \{0, 1\}\). A method sees the labels on a fixed training set \(S \subset \mathcal X\) of \(n < N\) points and returns a classifier \(\hat h\), which may be any function of those \(n\) labels. Its error on the points it has not seen is \[ \mathrm{err}(\hat h, f) = \frac{1}{N - n} \sum_{x \notin S} \mathbb 1\big[\hat h(x) \ne f(x)\big]. \]

  1. Draw \(f\) uniformly at random from all \(2^N\) labelings. Show that \(\mathbb E\big[\mathrm{err}(\hat h, f)\big] = \tfrac12\) for every method.

  2. Two methods, \(A\) and \(B\). Suppose \(A\)’s error on the unseen points is smaller than \(B\)’s for some labeling. Show that \(B\)’s is smaller than \(A\)’s for another labeling.

  3. What would have to be true of the world for some method to do better than \(\tfrac12\) on average? Where does that assumption sit in the ERM form of exercise 2?

NoteHint 1

Uniform over all \(2^N\) labelings means that the \(N\) labels \(f(x)\) are independent fair coin flips.

NoteHint 2

Fix \(x \notin S\). \(\hat h(x)\) depends only on the labels on \(S\), and \(f(x)\) is independent of them. Condition on the labels on \(S\): what is \(P\big(\hat h(x) \ne f(x)\big)\)?

NoteHint 3
  1. Average \(\mathrm{err}(\hat h_A, f) - \mathrm{err}(\hat h_B, f)\) over all \(2^N\) labelings.

Problems

Problem 1 (three observations). Three observations, \((x_i, y_i) = (-1, 0), (0, 1), (1, 3)\), a line \(\beta_0 + \beta_1 x\), and the objective \(\hat R_n(\beta) = \tfrac13\sum_i (y_i - \beta_0 - \beta_1 x_i)^2\).

  1. Compute the gradient of \(\hat R_n\) at \(\beta = (0, 0)\) and take one gradient-descent step with \(\gamma = 0.1\).

  2. At what \(\beta\) does the iteration stop moving, and which equations does that point satisfy?

  3. The curvatures of \(\hat R_n\) are the eigenvalues of \(2\mathbf X^T\mathbf X/n\). Compute them. What is the largest step size for which gradient descent converges from every start, and is \(\gamma = 0.5\) safe?

  4. Which coordinate converges more slowly with \(\gamma = 0.5\), and by what factor does its error shrink per step?

Problem 2 (one direction, then two). One direction with curvature \(a > 0\): \(f(\beta) = \tfrac12 a\beta^2\).

  1. Show that gradient descent with step \(\gamma\) gives \(\beta_t = (1 - \gamma a)^t\beta_0\), and find all \(\gamma\) for which it converges.

  2. Which \(\gamma\) reaches the minimum in one step?

  3. Two directions with curvatures \(10\) and \(1\) and one shared step size. Which \(\gamma\) makes the slower of the two directions shrink fastest, and what is the shrinkage factor per step then?

Problem 3 (a random row). \(\hat R_n(\beta) = \tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n \ell_i(\beta)\). Pick an index \(I\) uniformly at random from \(\{1, \dots, n\}\), with the data and \(\beta\) held fixed.

  1. Show that \(\mathbb E_I[\nabla\ell_I(\beta)] = \nabla\hat R_n(\beta)\). Which assumption about where the data came from did you use?

  2. A batch of \(b\) indices drawn independently with replacement, gradient averaged: give its mean and its variance in terms of \(\sigma^2(\beta) = \mathrm{Var}_I[\nabla\ell_I(\beta)]\) (one coordinate at a time is fine).

  3. If the batch is drawn without replacement, is the variance larger or smaller, and what is it when \(b = n\)?

  4. The descent lemma says a full gradient step of size \(1/L\) decreases \(\hat R_n\). Does a step of size \(1/L\) along \(\nabla\ell_I(\beta)\) decrease \(\hat R_n\)? Give a reason or a counterexample.

Problem 4 (the valley with shrinking steps). Gradient descent on the lecture’s valley, \(f(\beta) = \tfrac12(\beta_1^2 + 10\beta_2^2)\), from \(\beta_0 = (-4, 1.2)\) with the step sizes \(\gamma_t = 0.3 \cdot 0.6^t\), \(t = 1, 2, \dots\) (panel C of the lecture).

  1. Show that each coordinate obeys \(\beta_{j, t} = \beta_{j, 0}\prod_{s=1}^t (1 - a_j\gamma_s)\) with \(a_1 = 1\), \(a_2 = 10\).

  2. For the first coordinate, compute the first four factors and say what \(\beta_{1, t}\) converges to (two decimals are enough). Is it the minimizer?

  3. Compute \(\sum_{t \ge 1}\gamma_t\). Which of the two conditions on step sizes from the lecture does this schedule violate, and what does the sum say about how far the iterate can travel?
